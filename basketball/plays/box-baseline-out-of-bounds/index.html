Box Baseline Out-of-Bounds

The box set is the most common baseline out-of-bounds alignment in basketball. Four players line up at the blocks and elbows, so the defense has to guard every screen at once. This version uses a screen across for a layup, then the screener gets a screen of her own for a shot at the elbow, with a safety to the corner every time.

How to run it

  1. Box set. On the slap of the ball, 4 screens across for 5, who cuts to the rim. 2 pops to the corner as the safety.
  2. Screen the screener: 3 screens down for 4, who comes to the middle of the free-throw line for the shot.
  3. Inbound reads in order: 5 at the rim, 4 at the free-throw line, 2 in the corner. Here 5 is open.

Coaching points

  • Everyone breaks on the same signal, usually the inbounder slapping the ball.
  • The inbounder stands a step back from the line so she can see the whole floor.
  • Screens are set on a body, not on air. Hold them for a full count.
  • The safety (2) always gets open, so you never burn a timeout or give up a five-second call.

Reads

  • Screen across is open: bounce pass to the rim.
  • Defense switches the screen: the screener (4) seals and is open.
  • Everything is covered: safety pass to 2 in the corner and get into your offense.
